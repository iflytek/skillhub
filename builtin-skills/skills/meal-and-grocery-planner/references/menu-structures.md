# Menu Structures

Pick a **structure** before picking dishes. A structure guarantees variety, equipment compatibility
and fewer shopping surprises; a list of recipes guarantees three dishes that all need the oven at
190C.

## Structure by scene

### Weeknight, 25-30 minutes, one cook

Rotation of four roles, one per night, repeating:

1. **Pan dish** - protein + quick vegetable, one pan, 15 min hands-on.
2. **Tray dish** - everything on one tray, oven does the work, 35-40 min total.
3. **Assembly** - cold or barely cooked: grains + something rich + something fresh, 12 min.
4. **Batch base night** - cook double of one component (grains, sauce, braise) that feeds the next
   two days; tonight's meal is the fresh part only.

Why it works: at most two dishes need the hob on any night, one needs the oven, and the batch base
kills the "nothing in the fridge" failure on day 4.

### Weekend, 60-90 minutes, two cooks

- One oven centrepiece + one cold side + one starch. Cold side can be made in advance.
- Add one "project" element only if it can be done while the centrepiece cooks (a sauce, a pickle).
- Never plan two dishes that both need last-minute plating.

### Dinner party, 6-10 guests

- **One dish that scales** (a braise, a traybake, a big grain salad) instead of four dishes that
  each need attention.
- **One dish served at room temperature** so the timing has slack.
- **One thing bought** (bread, cheese, dessert) - it buys 20 minutes of host time.
- Number of dishes <= number of burners + 1. The oven counts as one burner.

### Batch cooking, 2-3 hours, one session

- Two proteins, two grains, one sauce, one roasted vegetable tray, one raw vegetable.
- Everything must be combinable in at least three ways, or it becomes repetitive by Wednesday.
- Cool fast, portion before storing, label with the date `[verify]` storage duration with the local
  food-safety authority.

## Equipment conflict rules

- **One oven** = one temperature. Group dishes by temperature; if two need different temperatures,
  one becomes a hob dish.
- **Two burners** = at most two simultaneous pan dishes; a third means one waits (and usually
  overcooks).
- **One pot** = no pasta-plus-sauce in parallel; use a one-pot or a cold dish.
- **No oven** = no traybakes, no roasting; replace with pan-roast, steam, or a cold dish.
- **Small fridge** = no 5-day braise plus a week of produce; shop twice.

State the conflict when you see it: "this menu needs three burners at 18:40, you have two".

## Repetition rules

- No dish repeats within 3 days unless it is batch cooking on purpose.
- No single ingredient as the *centre* of two dinners in a row (chicken Monday and chicken Tuesday
  reads as lazy even when it is efficient).
- Reuse of *components* is good (a sauce, a grain); reuse of *dishes* is not.

## Scaling rules

- Portions scale linearly up to about 2x; beyond that, pan crowding changes the cooking (steam
  instead of sear). Say so when scaling past 2x.
- Bigger batch means longer cooking, not the same time: add 30-50% time above 2x.
- For 8+ eaters, cook two trays rather than one deep one; surface area beats volume.

## Presenting

One table, one row per meal, columns `Day/Meal | Dish | Serves | Time | Equipment | Reuses | Why it
fits`. The `Reuses` column is what makes the plan credible - it shows the shopping list will be
short.
