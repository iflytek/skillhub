# Substitutions

The rule: **do not assume a limit; provide the swap.** Someone at the table may be vegetarian,
avoid dairy, avoid gluten, or eat halal - but unless the user says so, guessing changes the menu
for everyone. Offer swaps in a separate section and keep the main menu coherent.

## Vegetarian / vegan

| Ingredient | Swap | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Minced meat | Brown lentils (cooked, same volume), or crumbled firm tofu | less fat, add umami (soy, tomato paste, mushroom) |
| Chicken breast | Firm tofu or king oyster mushroom | cook hotter and shorter; tofu needs pressing |
| Stock (meat) | Vegetable stock | lower body; add a little extra fat |
| Butter | Olive oil or plant butter | browning is faster, watch the heat |
| Yoghurt (as a sauce base) | Coconut yoghurt or silken tofu blended | thinner, add acid |
| Cheese on top | Nutritional yeast, or omit and add toasted nuts | loses the melty pull |
| Cream | Coconut cream or cashew cream | sweeter, use less |
| Honey | Sugar or maple syrup | negligible in cooked dishes |
| Fish sauce | Soy + a squeeze of lime | loses the fermented depth |
| Egg as binder | Flax or chia slurry | denser texture |

## Dairy-free

Milk → oat or soy (soy for savoury, oat for sweet). Butter → oil or plant butter (reduce salt).
Cheese → in a baked dish, replace with a crumb topping; in a salad, replace with avocado or nuts.
Yoghurt → coconut yoghurt (check it is unsweetened).

## Gluten-free

Pasta → rice or corn pasta (cooks faster, do not overcook). Bread → check the label; a gluten-free
loaf behaves badly for toast, use it as croutons instead. Soy sauce → tamari. Flour as thickener →
cornflour at half the quantity. Stock cubes and sauces are the hidden sources - read the label
`[verify]`.

**Cross-contact matters more than ingredients**: shared toaster, shared oil, shared board. Say so
without prescribing; the person with the allergy decides.

## Halal / kosher / no pork / no alcohol

- Pork → lamb, beef or chicken in the same dish; the cooking time changes little in braises.
- Alcohol in a recipe (wine in a braise) → stock + a splash of vinegar; do not claim a "burn-off"
  equivalence as fact `[verify]`.
- Gelatine, rennet, certain emulsifiers are hidden sources - flag to read labels `[verify]`.
- Do not opine on whether a kitchen is certified; that is for the person and their authority.

## Allergen discipline

List the common allergens **present in the menu** in the brief: gluten, dairy, egg, fish, shellfish,
nuts, peanuts, soy, sesame. Say "contains" - not "may contain". If the user has an allergy in the
household, treat every ingredient as needing a label check `[verify]`.

Never say a dish is "safe for" someone. Say what it contains and let them decide.

## Substitution cost, stated

Every swap gets one clause on what it costs: texture, time, or flavour. "Lentils instead of mince -
adds 10 min and needs more salt" is useful; "you can use lentils" is not.

## Presentation

| Dish | Contains | Vegetarian swap | Dairy-free swap | Gluten-free swap | Cost of the swap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Keep it to the dishes that actually contain the allergen. A table covering everything reads as
boilerplate and gets skipped.
