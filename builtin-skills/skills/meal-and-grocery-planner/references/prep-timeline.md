# Prep Timeline

The clock is the difference between a plan and a dinner that lands at 21:40. Build it backwards from
serving time, and make parallel work explicit.

## Building the clock

1. **Fix serve time.** Everything is derived from it.
2. **Place the long items first.** Oven dishes, braises, grains and anything that holds well go
   early; they tolerate waiting.
3. **Place the fragile items last.** Fried, seared, dressed salads, anything with a crust.
4. **Assign equipment per slot.** Write `oven`, `burner 1`, `burner 2`, `board`, `fridge`. If two
   items claim the same slot, one moves.
5. **Insert holding rules.** What waits in a low oven, what waits at room temperature, what must be
   served immediately.
6. **Add a 10-minute buffer** before serving. Plans without a buffer are schedules, not plans.

## Typical dinner-party clock (serve 19:30, oven + 2 burners)

| When | Task | Equipment | Hands-on | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17:30 | Braise into the oven | oven 160C | 15 min | longest item, holds indefinitely |
| 17:45 | Grains on | burner 1 | 5 min | holds warm, frees the burner later |
| 18:10 | Cold side assembled, into fridge | board | 15 min | improves with resting |
| 18:45 | Vegetable tray roasted | oven 200C | 10 min | needs the oven after the braise drops to hold |
| 19:00 | Dressing mixed, table set | board | 10 min | no equipment conflict |
| 19:15 | Bread out, drinks poured | - | 5 min | guests arrive |
| 19:25 | Sear the fragile item | burner 2 | 8 min | last, served immediately |
| 19:30 | Serve | - | - | |

## Weeknight clock (serve 19:00, one pan, 30 min total)

| When | Task | Equipment | Hands-on | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:30 | Water on / oven preheat | burner 1 | 2 min | dead time used |
| 18:32 | Chop everything, one board | board | 10 min | mise en place prevents burning |
| 18:42 | Protein in the pan | burner 1 | 3 min | highest-heat item first |
| 18:50 | Vegetables in, grains drained | burner 1 | 5 min | shares the pan |
| 18:57 | Sauce, plate | board | 3 min | |

## Parallelism rules

- **One person can actively watch two things** at most; a third is a queue.
- **Oven time is free time** - that is why traybakes win on weeknights.
- **The board is a resource** like a burner: chopping while something sears is real parallelism,
  chopping while something deep-fries is not.
- **Cold dishes first** so they are chilled, not the other way round.
- **Batch base day**: cook the base, cool it fast (spread thin, shallow container), portion, then
  build tonight's dish from the fresh part only.

## Holding and reheating practice

- Keep hot food hot until serving; keep cold food cold. Do not leave food in the "middle" zone.
- Cool leftovers quickly before refrigerating - shallow containers, not one deep pot.
- Reheat thoroughly, not gently. Numbers for temperatures and storage durations come from the local
  food-safety authority `[verify]`; do not quote them from memory.
- Fish, rice and shellfish are the items to treat least casually on storage `[verify]`.

## Presentation

The clock table is for the heaviest day only. For weeknights, one line each is enough
(`18:30 water on, 18:42 protein in the pan, 19:00 serve`). A clock table for every day of the week
is noise and will not be read.
