# Emergency Sheet

Five failure classes, each with the **first signal** you can actually observe, who acts, and a
fallback whose cost is capped by the contingency. A fallback that costs more than the contingency
is not a fallback - it is a second risk.

## Failure classes

### 1. Headcount overshoot / no-shows

- First signal: RSVP rate diverges from the attendance pattern at T-3, or the door queue grows
  faster than check-in.
- Owner: door.
- Fallback: standing tolerance for the room (seats + 0.6 x headcount), extra glasses and ice from
  the nearest supplier, food top-up order with a 30-minute lead if the vendor allows `[verify]`.
- Cost cap: one contingency tranche. Above that, cap entry - naming the cap is the plan.

### 2. Food late or short

- First signal: vendor not on site 45 minutes before service, or a count mismatch on delivery.
- Owner: food.
- Fallback: the pre-identified nearby supplier, or a "cold buffer" (bread, cheese, fruit, snacks)
  bought in advance; reorder the run of show so the content block starts earlier while food lands.
- Cost cap: fixed buffer budget, decided at planning time.

### 3. AV / power failure

- First signal: no signal on the projector at T-30, or a device that will not charge.
- Owner: AV.
- Fallback: laptop swap, adapter set, printed backup of the deck, a room layout without slides
  (circle or standing), a speaker who can talk without the deck.
- Cost cap: zero if the kit list was followed; the real cost is the content compromise - name it.

### 4. Key person absent

- First signal: no confirmation at T-1, or a late cancellation on the day.
- Owner: content (for speakers), comms (for the announcement).
- Fallback: a named understudy, a shortened agenda, or a replaced block with a discussion format.
  Never leave a gap unfilled: an unfilled 20 minutes is what people remember.
- Cost cap: zero; this is a decision, not a purchase.

### 5. Weather (outdoor only)

- First signal: forecast change at T-2 and again at T-6h; wind is the harder constraint.
- Owner: venue.
- Fallback: covered area or indoor room held as an option `[verify]`, shortened format, or a
  postponement date agreed in writing at planning time.
- Cost cap: the option-hold fee, decided before the event.

## Format-specific sixth row

Add one more for the specific format: hybrid (stream failure), seated dinner (course timing),
workshop (materials shortfall), family event (children's supervision), press event (statement
approval). One row, one fallback - not a register.

## How to present

| Failure | First signal | Who acts | Fallback | Cost cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Keep it to five or six rows. Each row must be actionable by a person who did not write the plan.
If a fallback needs a phone number, write "the venue contact on the contract" instead of inventing
a number.

## Rules

1. Every fallback has an **owner role**, not a name.
2. Every fallback has a **cost cap**; where the cap is zero, say what is lost instead (content,
   comfort, timing).
3. Never write a fallback that requires a booking made on the day - lead times do not exist on
   the day.
4. Re-read the sheet at T-1 with the staff brief. An emergency sheet nobody read is decoration.
