# Regulated and Restricted Items

This file lists **what to check and who to ask**. It deliberately contains no numeric limit,
because limits change, vary by carrier, vary by country and are frequently tightened without
notice. Every row is `[verify]`.

| Category | Why it is regulated | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium batteries / power banks | Watt-hour caps, carry-on-only rules, terminal markings | the airline's dangerous-goods page + the security agency of the departure airport |
| Spare lithium cells | Must be carry-on, terminal-protected | airline |
| Liquids and aerosols | Volume per container and per bag, screening exceptions | airport security agency |
| Sharp objects | Blade length and type, tools, nail scissors | airport security agency |
| Medication | Prescription proof, controlled substances, quantity for the trip length | the destination's customs/health authority + the prescribing clinician |
| Injectables and cold-chain | Documentation, transport temperature | clinician + airline special assistance |
| Food and agricultural products | Meat, dairy, seeds, fresh fruit, honey | destination customs/quarantine |
| Plants, soil, wooden items | Quarantine risk | destination customs |
| Drones | Registration, weight class, no-fly zones, batteries | civil aviation authority of the destination + airline |
| E-cigarettes and vape liquid | Often restricted or banned; battery rules | airline + destination authority |
| Alcohol and tobacco | Duty-free allowance, seals, transit rules | destination customs |
| Cash and negotiable instruments | Declaration thresholds | destination customs |
| Professional/broadcast gear | Carnet, permits, filming permission | destination customs + local film authority |
| Sporting weapons, flare, gas canister | Almost always forbidden or special-handled | airline dangerous goods |
| Pet and animal transport | Documentation, quarantine, carrier rules | airline + destination veterinary authority |

## How to present it

Only include a row if the corresponding item is actually in the packing list. Format:

| Item | Rule area | Authority to check | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power bank 20,000mAh | lithium battery carry-on rule | airline dangerous-goods page `[verify]` | confiscation at security, missed flight |
| Prescription medicine, 10 days | medication documentation | destination customs `[verify]` | seizure at border |
| Hiking knife | sharp object / checked-bag rule | airport security `[verify]` | confiscation, secondary screening |

## Rules of engagement

1. **Never state a limit as current fact.** "Usually 100ml" is still a claim that can be wrong
   for the specific airport; write `[verify]` and name the authority.
2. **Never advise concealment.** If an item is restricted, the answer is a different item or a
   checked-bag/rental/destination-purchase route, not a workaround.
3. **Never advise on medical necessity or dosing.** Point to the clinician and the customs rule.
4. **Transit counts.** A rule at the destination may not apply at the transit point; when the
   route has a connection, say so and treat it as an additional check.
5. **Say which items can simply be left behind.** Most regulated friction disappears by not
   carrying the object: rent the drone, buy the sunscreen, use the hotel hair dryer.

## Presentation budget

Keep the table under ~8 rows. A regulated table that lists everything reads as boilerplate and
gets skipped - which is the failure mode this section exists to prevent.
